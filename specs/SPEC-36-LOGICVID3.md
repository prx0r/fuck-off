# The beautiful thing

This is almost exactly how great theoretical physics has progressed.

Take symmetry.

Physicists didn't decide symmetry was important.

They kept solving unrelated problems and discovering symmetry waiting underneath them.

Or take information.

It appeared independently in:

* communications,
* thermodynamics,
* black holes,
* quantum mechanics,
* biology,
* neuroscience.

Its importance came from **repeated convergence**, not because someone declared it fundamental.

---

I think there's one final rule I'd add, because it's what makes your approach different from a concept map:

> **Never create a primitive node because it sounds important. Only create it when multiple independent investigations force it into existence.**

That prevents the graph from filling up with fashionable abstractions. "Consciousness" or "reality" don't get privileged because they're grand words. They earn their place only if many unrelated paradoxes converge on them and they survive repeated reduction attempts.

That's a very different epistemology from most philosophy. It's closer to how mathematics and physics discover deep structures: not by choosing them first, but by finding that entirely different routes keep arriving at the same place.
