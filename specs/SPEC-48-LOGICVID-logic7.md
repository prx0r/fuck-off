The key is to separate three things that your current formulation is beginning to collapse:

[
R=\text{reality/process}
]

[
K=\text{cognition or adaptive world-modelling}
]

[
M=\text{manifestation: the fact that anything appears}

The scientific chain increasingly supports:

[
R\longrightarrow K

But it has not established:

[
K=M

or:

[
K\longrightarrow M.

That unresolved relation is the frontier.

# 1. The bottle is a controlled construction

Seth’s “controlled hallucination” does not mean that the bottle is imaginary. It means that perceptual content is generated by a hierarchical model whose predictions are continuously constrained by sensory input. Perception is neither passive copying nor unconstrained fantasy:

[
\text{perception}
=================

\text{top-down prediction}
+
\text{bottom-up error correction}.
]

The bottle you experience is therefore the brain’s best continuously updated hypothesis concerning the hidden causes of its sensory signals. Seth’s more technical work also argues that an object’s felt **perceptual presence** depends on counterfactual sensorimotor predictions: the system implicitly expects how sensory input would change if the eyes, head or body moved. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][1])

So the bottle is not the final frame of a linear pipeline:

```text
photons → retina → brain → bottle
```

It is a temporarily stable attractor within a circular process:

```text
world
  ↓
sensory constraint
  ↓
generative prediction
  ↓
action
  ↓
changed world-sampling
  ↺
```

More formally:

[
K_t=(m_t,s_t,a_t,\varepsilon_t)
]

where the model (m_t), sensory input (s_t), action (a_t), and prediction error (\varepsilon_t) continuously transform one another.

There may therefore be no fundamental instant called:

[
\text{“the cognition of the bottle.”}
]

There is a temporally extended organism–world trajectory that can be usefully coarse-grained as one perception.

# 2. Hoffman is relevant—but his claim is stronger

Hoffman agrees that the perceived bottle is an interface object rather than a transparent depiction of objective reality.

His Interface Theory of Perception argues, using evolutionary game-theoretic models, that selection optimizes perceptions for fitness rather than literal truth. The bottle is analogous to a desktop icon: it guides successful action without resembling the underlying reality with which the organism interacts. ([Association for Psychological Science][2])

But two corrections matter.

## Hoffman does not simply say everything is one quantum conscious field

His formal primitive is a **conscious agent**, represented using measurable spaces and Markov kernels for:

* perception;
* decision;
* action.

A simplified agent has the structure:

[
C=(X,G,P,D,A,N),
]

where (X) is a space of experiences, (G) a space of actions, and (P,D,A) are probabilistic channels connecting world, experience and action. Agents can be composed into networks, and agent–world interaction can be redescribed as agent–agent interaction. ([Frontiers][3])

The ontology proposed is:

[
\text{network of conscious agents}
]

rather than one ordinary conscious agent.

Hoffman and collaborators propose that spacetime and physical objects are interface representations of those deeper dynamics. They have identified mathematical correspondences between asymptotic conscious-agent dynamics and wave-function structures, but they have **not derived the Standard Model, quantum field theory and general relativity as a completed result**. The 2024 statement of the programme still presents the recovery of spacetime and physical structure as ongoing theoretical work. ([Frontiers][3])

## The mathematics does not prove consciousness

Calling (X) a space of conscious experiences is an interpretation placed on the formalism.

Markov kernels can model many non-conscious stochastic systems. The mathematics gives:

[
\text{probabilistic perception–decision–action dynamics}.
]

It does not independently entail:

[
\text{phenomenal consciousness}.
]

That is Hoffman’s foundational ontological postulate, not a theorem derived from the kernels.

# 3. Levin supplies the missing scale transition

Levin’s work directly addresses your question:

> How do millions of competent subunits become one larger agent with a stable world and larger goals?

His answer is not that cells possess human-style bottle experiences. It is that cognition should be understood as a graded capacity for:

* sensing;
* memory;
* preference;
* goal-directed error correction;
* navigating possible state spaces;
* coordinating action.

Cells regulate physiological variables. Tissues coordinate morphological outcomes. Organisms navigate behavioural space. The same broad control architecture can operate at several scales. ([PubMed][4])

The crucial transition is:

[
\text{many local goal spaces}
\longrightarrow
\text{one enlarged goal space}.
]

Bioelectric communication helps cells share information and coordinate around anatomical targets that no isolated cell represents completely. In Levin’s framework, a multicellular “self” is partly defined by the scale of the states it can measure, remember and work to control. ([PubMed][5])

So the human bottle-perception depends on nested competencies:

```text
molecular regulation
→ cellular homeostasis
→ tissue coordination
→ nervous-system integration
→ sensorimotor prediction
→ conceptual categorization
→ report: “bottle”
```

These levels are not merely stacked. Higher-level goals constrain lower-level activity, while lower-level activity enables higher-level cognition.

The experienced bottle is therefore not simply produced “by neurons.” It depends upon a history including:

* evolution;
* embryogenesis;
* bodily regulation;
* sensorimotor learning;
* social learning;
* language;
* immediate world-coupling.

Levin supplies a theory of how **agency scales**.

He does not yet supply a theory of why scaled agency is phenomenally manifest.

# 4. The most important correction: cognition need not equal manifestation

You suggested:

> If the process is active, perhaps manifestation belongs to none of its parts. Maybe manifestation itself is cognition.

That gives a serious candidate:

[
\boxed{M=K}
]

Manifestation would not be an extra substance attached to cognitive processing. It would be what sufficiently organized, self-maintaining cognition is “from within.”

This family includes functionalist, enactivist and some dual-aspect approaches.

But there are two very different versions.

## Identity version

[
M\equiv K.
]

Phenomenal manifestation just is the integrated cognitive process, although it may be described differently from the first-person and third-person perspectives.

Then asking:

> “Why does cognition generate manifestation?”

would be like asking:

> “Why does lightning generate electrical discharge?”

It does not generate it. They are the same event under two descriptions.

## Emergence version

[
K\longrightarrow M.
]

Once nested cognition crosses some organizational threshold, manifestation emerges.

This requires identifying the relevant threshold:

* recurrent processing;
* global availability;
* integration;
* self-modelling;
* counterfactual depth;
* temporally extended causal unity;
* biological autonomy.

No current theory has established a generally accepted sufficient condition. Contemporary consciousness research instead offers competing indicator properties, and predictive processing by itself does not distinguish all conscious from unconscious inference. ([arXiv][6])

# 5. “Manifestation is cognition from the observer’s perspective”

This formulation contains a hidden circularity.

Suppose:

[
M=\text{cognition as experienced by an observer}.
]

Then who or what is the observer?

## Option A: another cognitive process

Then:

[
K_1
\text{ is manifest to }
K_2.
]

But what makes (K_2) manifest?

You risk regress:

[
K_1\leftarrow K_2\leftarrow K_3\leftarrow\dots
]

## Option B: the whole system observes its parts

Then consciousness belongs to the global pattern:

[
M(\Gamma)
]

rather than any individual component.

This is coherent, but it needs a non-arbitrary rule specifying which global processes count as unified observers.

## Option C: observer is merely perspectival language

There is no extra inner observer. “From the inside” and “from the outside” are two descriptions of one process.

This avoids regress, but it still owes an account of why the process admits a first-person description at all.

Thus:

[
\boxed{
\text{observer cannot explain manifestation unless observerhood is independently explained.}
}
]

# 6. Enactivism may fit your intuition better than predictive processing alone

Predictive processing can still sound as though the brain constructs an internal bottle model and then consults it.

Enactivism removes that residual inner-theatre picture.

On enactive and ecological accounts, cognition is constituted through the dynamically coupled organism–environment system. The environment is not merely input; organism and environment jointly determine the perceptual domain. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][7])

Then cognition is not located exclusively:

* inside the neurons;
* inside the cells;
* or outside in the bottle.

It is the structured relation:

[
K=
\operatorname{Dynamics}
(
\text{organism},
\text{body},
\text{environment},
\text{history}
).
]

The bottle is an enacted affordance:

> something graspable, drinkable, movable and stable under possible actions.

That comes extremely close to Seth’s account of perceptual presence as encoded counterfactual sensorimotor possibilities. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][1])

# 7. Wallace gives the best model of ontological levels

Wallace’s relevance is methodological.

In Everettian quantum mechanics, macroscopic objects and outcomes need not be fundamental entities written directly into the basic equations. They can be real patterns supported by decohering structure.

Applied here:

[
\text{cells}
]

[
\text{organisms}
]

[
\text{selves}
]

[
\text{bottles}
]

[
\text{perceptions}
]

can all be real without being fundamental or sharply bounded.

The criterion is not indivisibility. It is whether a pattern is sufficiently stable, dynamically autonomous and explanatorily indispensable.

So:

[
\boxed{
\text{The absence of an exact cognition boundary does not make cognition unreal.}
}
]

A hurricane lacks a single atomic boundary but is still a real dynamical pattern.

Likewise:

[
\text{“seeing the bottle”}
]

can be real without occurring at one exact neuron, instant or representational frame.

This helps resolve the **individuation problem**.

It does not resolve manifestation.

# 8. Tegmark reaches structure but not appearing

Tegmark’s Mathematical Universe Hypothesis states that physical reality is an abstract mathematical structure. His later “perceptronium” proposal asks which structural properties—such as integration, dynamics, independence and information processing—might distinguish conscious systems and explain why observers experience a world divided into relatively autonomous objects. ([arXiv][8])

This is relevant to your nested-pattern ontology:

[
\text{reality}
==============

\text{mathematical relational structure}
]

[
\text{observer}
===============

\text{self-aware substructure}
]

[
\text{experienced objects}
==========================

\text{factorization selected by the observer’s organization}.
]

But “self-aware substructure” is doing the unexplained work.

Mathematical structure can specify:

* relations;
* transformations;
* factorization;
* information;
* causal organization.

It does not obviously specify:

[
\text{why the structure appears}.
]

This is precisely where Tegmark reaches Abhinavagupta’s pressure point and stops.

# 9. The five ontologies now available

Your inquiry has generated five serious positions.

## A. Predictive functionalism

[
R\rightarrow K=M.
]

Reality produces adaptive predictive processes; sufficiently organized cognition simply is manifestation.

Problem:

> Which organization is sufficient, and why?

## B. Emergent process consciousness

[
R\rightarrow K\rightarrow M.
]

Nested biological cognition eventually generates manifestation.

Problem:

> How does non-manifest process cross into manifest process?

## C. Hoffman-style conscious realism

[
M+A\rightarrow R_{\text{interface}}.
]

Conscious agency is primitive; perceived physical reality is an adaptive interface over deeper conscious-agent dynamics.

Problem:

> Why call the formal agents conscious, and can spacetime physics actually be recovered?

## D. Neutral or dual-aspect structuralism

[
U\rightarrow
\begin{cases}
R\
M
\end{cases}
]

One underlying process has structural/causal and phenomenal aspects.

Problem:

> What determines the correspondence between the aspects?

## E. Pratyabhijñā

[
M\rightarrow K\rightarrow R_{\text{determinate}}.
]

Manifest consciousness is primitive. Cognition is its active self-determination, and apparently external reality is a structured manifestation within it.

Problem:

> Why must manifestation be active, unified and universal rather than local or impersonal?

# 10. Abhinavagupta becomes more cutting—but must be reformulated

The weak version of his claim is:

> Every cognition belongs to consciousness.

The stronger process-compatible version is:

[
\boxed{
\text{Every differentiation, process, model and observer occurs as manifestation.}
}
]

He need not deny:

* nested biological construction;
* predictive inference;
* emergent object patterns;
* collective cellular intelligence;
* organism–environment coupling.

He can accept all of them as descriptions of:

[
\textit{vimarśa}
]

the active differentiation and self-articulation of manifestation.

The distinction becomes:

[
\textit{prakāśa}
================

\text{manifest actuality}
]

[
\textit{vimarśa}
================

\text{its active, relational, differentiating structure}
]

[
\textit{spanda}
===============

\text{the intrinsic dynamism through which unity articulates difference}.
]

Science may increasingly explain the structure of *vimarśa*:

* how distinctions stabilize;
* how goals scale;
* how agents emerge;
* how interfaces form;
* how objects become actionable;
* how self-models are maintained.

But reorganizing those structures does not automatically explain *prakāśa*.

That yields the exact contention:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{Is manifestation an additional fact about cognitive organization, or is “cognitive organization” simply the third-person abstraction of manifestation’s own activity?}
}
]

# 11. What has not been figured out

Several pieces are becoming clearer:

### Object formation

Predictive processing and enactivism explain how stable, action-guiding worlds are constructed.

### Agent formation

Levin explains mechanisms through which smaller competencies can combine into larger goal-directed selves.

### Ontological levels

Wallace explains how emergent patterns can be fully real without being fundamental.

### Interface opacity

Hoffman formalizes the possibility that adaptive perception need not resemble underlying reality.

### Structural fundamentality

Tegmark explores reality and observers as mathematical structures.

But no accepted theory currently unifies:

[
\text{pattern individuation}
+
\text{agency}
+
\text{phenomenal manifestation}
+
\text{first-person unity}.
]

That is the frontier.

# The deepest dependency you have exposed

The question is no longer:

> How does the brain construct a bottle?

It is:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{What makes one nested process a perspective from which a bottle manifests?}
}
]

That contains four unresolved subproblems:

[
\text{individuation:}
\quad
\text{Why is this one process?}
]

[
\text{agency:}
\quad
\text{Why does it possess goals?}
]

[
\text{perspectivity:}
\quad
\text{Why is the world organized relative to it?}
]

[
\text{manifestation:}
\quad
\text{Why does that organization appear?}
]

Levin attacks individuation and agency.

Seth attacks perspectival world-construction.

Hoffman reverses the relation between interface and ontology.

Wallace explains real emergent patternhood.

Abhinavagupta attacks manifestation itself.

The next Logicvid should therefore be:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{What makes a process a point of view?}
}
]

Not merely one agent.

Not merely one integrated system.

A **point of view**: a boundary from which one actionable world becomes manifest.

That is the basin where all five branches have now converged.
